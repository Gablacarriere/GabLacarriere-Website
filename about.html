About Gab Lacarriere

Dance artist. Educator. Movement thinker.

Gab Lacarriere is a Brazilian Zouk and Lambada artist, educator and DJ based in New York City. His work combines social dance, pedagogy, movement research and creative expression.

Biography

For Gab, technique is not the end goal. It is the structure that makes freedom possible.

Originally from France, Gab came to dance with a background in pedagogy and philosophy of science, together with professional experience as a school teacher. That foundation continues to shape his approach to movement education through observation, diagnosis, sequencing, feedback and learning design.

Gab began teaching Brazilian Zouk in France around 2017–2018, first in Rennes and later in Paris. His early professional years included a long-running collaboration with Sabrina Beija-Flor, combining regular Brazilian Zouk classes, workshops, social events and European appearances, alongside his developing work as DJ Gab.

He later developed a teaching and performance partnership with Patrycja Jakimczyk, teaching Brazilian Zouk and Lambada in Paris and appearing at workshops, festivals and dance events across Europe.

In 2023, Gab began collaborating with Danish dancer and educator Caroline Haugsted. They became official dance partners in 2024 and developed Zouk Art Empowerment, an approach combining technical clarity with creative exploration, expression and embodied connection. Their work included teaching and international projects across Europe, Mexico and South America.

Now based in New York City, Gab teaches Brazilian Zouk and Lambada through weekly classes, workshops, private coaching, Kinesthetic Practice Sessions and long-term mentorship. His current teaching focuses on movement architecture, timing, connection, body organization, deliberate practice and dancer autonomy.

Alongside teaching, Gab works as a DJ, performer, model and movement-based creative artist.

Teaching philosophy

Technique creates options. Understanding creates independence. The goal is not to collect patterns, but to understand movement well enough to make choices.
