Workshops that teach more than a sequence.

Brazilian Zouk and Lambada workshops with Gab Lacarriere are built around transferable principles: movement architecture, connection, timing, body organization, partner communication and adaptability.

What organizers can expect

Clear pedagogy

Concepts are organized so dancers understand what creates the movement, not only what shape to reproduce.

High transfer

Exercises are designed to survive beyond the workshop and transfer into social dancing with different partners.

Adaptable level

Material can be shaped for developing dancers, experienced social dancers, competitors or mixed-level festival groups.

Workshop directions

Topics can be built around the needs of the event.

Movement Architecture

Understand how movements are built, connected and transformed so dancers can create rather than memorize.

Connection & Communication

Refine how information travels through the partnership and how both dancers organize around shared movement.

Timing & Rhythm

Use timing as a movement tool rather than a count to obey, with applications to both Zouk and Lambada.

Social-Dance Adaptability

Train decision-making, variation and partner adaptation so material becomes useful in real social dancing.

Technique Through Constraints

Use focused constraints and deliberate practice to isolate the skill that actually limits the movement.

Lambada Pathways

Develop rhythm, body organization, pathways and partner communication with a structure-first approach.

Teaching background

Gab's teaching career developed from France into international festival and workshop work, and is now based in New York City.

His professional collaborations have included Brazilian Zouk and Lambada teaching across European dance communities, as well as work in Mexico and South America. His current teaching combines movement education with a background in pedagogy and deliberate-practice design.

For organizers

Workshops can be designed as a single class, a progressive multi-class track, an intensive, or a weekend curriculum. Topics can be coordinated in advance around the event's level and learning goals.

Teaching principle

A good workshop should leave dancers with a tool they can still use after they forget the combination.
