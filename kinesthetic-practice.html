Movement-first one-on-one training

Less talking. More movement.

A Kinesthetic Practice Session is a guided one-on-one Zouk or Lambada practice focused on repetition, sensation, timing, connection and physical feedback rather than extended explanation.

Book a SessionCompare with Private Lessons
What it is

Practice with a partner who is actively shaping the learning environment.

The session is intentionally semi-limited: fewer explanations, more dancing, more repetition and more opportunities for the body to organize the skill.

Instead of stopping after every attempt to discuss what happened, Gab guides the practice through partner feedback, task constraints, timing changes, repetition and selective cues. The objective is to help the movement become more available without turning the session into a traditional private lesson.

The emphasis

Movement volume · physical feedback · timing · connection · coordination · repetition · movement habits.

Kinesthetic vs private lesson

Private lesson

Coaching-led. Diagnose a problem, explain it, build a model, train it and transfer it back into dancing.

Kinesthetic practice

Movement-led. Reduce discussion, increase dancing, repeat the skill and use physical feedback to shape the habit.

Which one?

If you need to understand why something is not working, choose a private. If you already understand it but need your body to absorb it, kinesthetic practice may be better.

What we can train

Connection

Refine how information is given, received and maintained through continuous partner practice.

Timing

Repeat movement under different timings until the coordination becomes more stable and adaptable.

Movement quality

Work on continuity, tone, weight organization, pathways and efficiency through sustained movement.

Social-dance fluency

Practice transitions, variation and decision-making with less stopping and more realistic dancing.

Lambada rhythm & pathways

Build physical familiarity with rhythm, body organization and pathways through repeated partnered movement.

Specific movement habits

Choose one constraint and accumulate high-quality repetitions until the body starts finding the solution more consistently.

The principle

Some things need to be explained. Other things need to be felt enough times that the body stops needing the explanation.

Who it is for

A good fit if…

You already have some technical understanding, but want more embodied repetition, better timing, stronger connection, smoother coordination or greater social-dance fluency.

Choose a private instead if…

You are unsure what the problem is, need detailed conceptual explanation, want a full technical diagnosis or need to rebuild the movement from first principles.
