# Gab Lacarriere Methodology — Default Movement / Sequence Class Architecture

**Status:** Canonical methodology component  
**Adopted:** 2026-09-15  
**Authority:** Explicit Gab methodology decision

This document defines the default architecture for classes whose main content is a movement, several movements, or a short sequence. It does not replace other class formats such as a dedicated cognitive, musicality, discussion, or teacher-training session.

## Core idea

A movement class should not be organized as “show a long sequence, repeat it, then correct it.”

The default logic is:

**GENERAL WARM-UP → SPECIFIC WARM-UP → SHOW THE DESTINATION → DECOMPOSE → FOR EACH MOVEMENT: SHOW → ATTEMPT → FIND THE PROBLEM → CHOOSE AN EXERCISE → FADE TEACHER SUPPORT → NEXT MOVEMENT → RECONNECT EVERYTHING**

The exercise follows the diagnosis. The teacher does not choose an exercise merely because it belongs to the movement vocabulary; the exercise should address the problem that actually appears in the students.

## 1. General warm-up

Raise overall physical readiness and move the body broadly before the technical demands of the class.

The general warm-up should involve the whole body and progressively increase readiness without already turning into the main technical lesson.

## 2. Specific warm-up

Prepare the exact demands of the class.

This can include targeted drills for the tissues, ranges of motion, timing, weight transfer, coordination, partner information, or movement mechanisms that will appear later.

Specific drills are therefore allowed—and often desirable—inside the warm-up when they prepare the later movement.

## 3. Show the destination

Briefly demonstrate the movements or complete sequence students are going to work toward.

Students should know what the parts will eventually become, but the teacher should avoid spending a long time explaining the entire sequence at once.

## 4. Decompose into a small number of movement units

Cut the sequence into useful movements and, when needed, cut each movement into smaller components.

For example:

**Basic Step → Elástico → Lateral**

These are then trained one at a time before being reconnected.

The number of movements should be limited by available class time. A movement that cannot receive demonstration, diagnosis, at least one useful exercise, sufficient repetitions, and integration should normally be removed from the class rather than rushed.

**Scope principle:** fewer movements with meaningful practice are preferable to more movements with insufficient learning time.

## 5. Per-movement teaching loop

Each movement normally receives its own learning loop.

### A. Show and isolate the movement

Demonstrate the movement clearly and place it in relation to the larger sequence.

### B. Let students attempt it

Give students a chance to try the movement before supplying every correction.

The first attempts provide diagnostic information.

### C. Identify the problem

Observe what is actually limiting the movement.

Possible problems can involve timing, counting, weight transfer, direction, connection, pathway, body organization, coordination, anticipation, range, or another constraint.

The most visible error is not automatically the causal problem.

### D. Problem → exercise

Use the identified problem to select or create an exercise.

**Default rule:** at least one targeted exercise should normally be available for each movement unit. More exercises can be used when multiple distinct bottlenecks justify them.

The exercise is a means of improving the movement, not a disconnected activity.

### E. Fade teacher support

After the exercise, rebuild the movement through progressively less teacher support.

Default progression:

1. **No music + Gab’s voice / counting / cues**
2. **Music + Gab’s voice / counting / cues**
3. **Music without Gab’s voice**

The exact verbal support can be counting, rhythm, movement cues, or another concise scaffold appropriate to the task.

Students should not progress merely because one repetition succeeded. Give enough repetitions for the movement to become more self-organized.

### F. Move to the next movement

Only then begin the same loop for the next movement in the sequence.

## 6. Reconnect the movements

After the movement units have been trained separately, reconnect them progressively.

For a sequence such as:

**Basic Step → Elástico → Lateral**

students first learn and improve each part, then connect the neighboring transitions, and finally rebuild the complete sequence.

If the sequence breaks during integration, diagnose whether the problem is:

- inside one of the movements; or
- specifically in the transition between movements.

Do not assume that repeating the whole sequence is the best solution.

The same support-fading logic can be reused during integration when necessary:

**without music + teacher voice → music + teacher voice → music without teacher voice.**

## 7. Practice density

The class should contain enough repetitions for the exercise and movement to have a real learning effect.

Teacher explanation should remain proportional to the problem. If a distinction can be established quickly, move into practice rather than filling time with explanation.

## 8. Teacher judgment

This architecture is a default, not a rigid script.

A movement may require more than one exercise, more time, a different progression, or fewer stages. A group may need only one or two movements in an entire class. Advanced or safety-sensitive material may require additional preparation and teacher-observed readiness.

The central rule remains:

**Observe the movement → identify the problem → choose the exercise → practice with progressively less support → integrate.**
