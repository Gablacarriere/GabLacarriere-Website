Celebrating 30 years of dance · 1996–2026

My mestre,
Alex de Carvalho.

In 2026, Alex celebrates 30 years of his dance career. With his anniversary celebration in Paris on September 26, I wanted to take a moment to honor my mestre and share what his teaching and generosity have meant to me.

The way I see Brazilian Zouk is deeply shaped by Alex. His teaching lives in my pedagogy, his generosity lives in my story, and I am proud to call him my mestre to this day.

A personal reflection by Gab Lacarriere · September 2026

01 · The beginning

He took my dream seriously.

I met Alex in 2017, when I began Brazilian Zouk. I had watched a video of William and Paloma, and it gave me a dream: one day, I wanted to become as good as William.

When I told Alex, he didn’t laugh. He told me to come and train with him, and that I would need to train a lot. He took that ambition seriously and showed me the work it would ask of me.

I still remember what that meant: to be at the beginning of something, with such a big dream, and to have a teacher make room for it.

02 · A way of seeing

His teaching is woven into mine.

My pedagogy is deeply imbued with Alex’s. His structured approach to Brazilian Zouk shaped how I understand the dance: how I look at movement, make sense of its structure, and organize what I teach.

I absorbed that approach and made it my own. Over time, it has met my background in education, my experience with students, and my own questions. The version I share today has my voice, but Alex’s influence runs through it.

For me, acknowledging that influence is part of being a teacher. When I explain my approach to Brazilian Zouk, I want people to know who helped me learn to see it this way.

03 · Beyond the studio

The doors he opened.

Alex’s support went beyond training. He paid for my flights so I could have opportunities to DJ at Zouk festivals in Europe, helping open doors that could also lead to teaching.

That generosity has stayed with me. He gave from the heart, from a teacher to a student, with a care that felt like a father’s for a son. I am deeply grateful for the opportunities he helped me find and the belief behind them.

I have developed my own voice.
His influence runs through it.

04 · Still my mestre

An artist who keeps learning.

Alex is one of the artists I respect most. I admire his dancing, his singing, and the way he continues to develop his art. His dance keeps evolving, and he remains open to learning from the younger generation.

That openness means a great deal to me. Even with everything he has contributed and everyone he has taught, he continues to be a student himself. It is a quality I admire in him as both an artist and a teacher.

We remain close today. Calling him my mestre describes a living relationship: the respect, affection, and gratitude I still feel, and the influence he continues to have on my work.

As I teach and develop my own approach, I want to carry that generosity forward—to take a student’s ambition seriously, make room for their growth, and keep learning myself.

Thank you, Alex.
Gab

Meet Alex

A life in Brazilian Zouk.

Alex began his dance career at Jaime Arôxa’s academy in 1996. His work has included leading dance schools in Brazil and Paris and training dance professionals internationally. He developed MAC, a methodology that breaks Brazilian Zouk movements into key elements to understand how the dance is built.

Gabriel Lacarriere is listed in the official MAC certified international instructor directory under reference 20180084.

His 30-year career celebration is announced for September 26, 2026, in Paris. View the anniversary celebration.
