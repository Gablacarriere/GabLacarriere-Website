Why the method exists

Learning more movements is not the same as becoming a better dancer.

The goal is to build perception, understanding, coordination and choice—not dependency on memorized patterns.

Training starts by identifying what organizes the movement: timing, connection, body organization, pathway, intention and decision-making. From there, practice is designed around the actual bottleneck.

Structure creates freedom

Once a principle is understood and embodied, it becomes easier to adapt it to new partners, timing, music and situations.

The learning cycle

1. Understand

Build a simple mental model of what matters instead of memorizing an isolated answer.

2. Feel

Translate the idea into timing, weight, direction, tone, connection and body organization.

3. Practice

Use focused repetition, retrieval and feedback around the exact skill that needs improvement.

4. Adapt

Change partner, timing, entry, exit, music and constraints until the skill survives variation.

Core principles

Diagnosis before correction

A visible mistake may come from timing, perception, organization, connection or decision-making.

Transfer over performance

A skill is not considered learned only because it works once in a drill. It must survive variation.

Autonomy

The long-term goal is a dancer who can perceive problems, understand movement and make informed choices independently.

The idea in one sentence

Teaching should progressively make the student more capable of learning, adapting and choosing.
