Teaching method

Understand. Feel.
Practice. Adapt.

A movement-education approach for Brazilian Zouk and Lambada. The aim is not to memorize more patterns, but to understand what organizes movement, practice the right problem, and become increasingly able to adapt without depending on the teacher.

See the learning cycleFor teachers →

The learning cycle

Four actions organize the method.

1 · Understand

Build a useful mental model of the movement: timing, pathway, connection, body organization and intention.

2 · Feel

Translate the explanation into weight, direction, tone, rhythm and partner information that can actually be sensed.

3 · Practice

Work on the specific bottleneck with focused repetition, retrieval, feedback and constraints.

4 · Adapt

Change partner, timing, entry, exit, music and context until the skill survives variation.

Inside real-time dancing

Learning is slow. Dancing is fast.

Inside a dance, the same pedagogy becomes a rapid decision loop:

Recognize → Visualize → Calculate → Choose → Execute → Update.

Recognize where you are in the unfolding pattern. Simulate possible continuations. Estimate when they arrive. Choose one, execute it, then update from the partner, music and result.

Core principles

Three rules guide the teaching decisions.

Diagnosis before correction

The visible mistake is not necessarily the cause. First identify whether the bottleneck is perception, timing, organization, connection or decision-making.

Transfer over performance

A drill is useful only if the skill becomes available outside the drill. Variation and changing constraints test whether learning transfers.

Autonomy over dependence

The long-term goal is a dancer who can notice problems, understand movement, choose practice and make informed decisions independently.
