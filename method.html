The Gab Lacarriere Method

Understand. Feel. Practice. Adapt.

A pedagogy-led approach to Brazilian Zouk and Lambada designed to help dancers move from imitation toward understanding, and from understanding toward autonomy.

Train through MentorshipPrivate Training
Why this method exists

Learning more movements is not the same as becoming a better dancer.

The goal is not to collect patterns. It is to build perception, understanding, coordination and choice.

Gab's teaching is shaped by pedagogy, movement analysis and deliberate practice. Instead of treating technique as a list of shapes to reproduce, training starts by identifying what organizes the movement: timing, connection, body organization, pathway, intention and decision-making.

Structure creates freedom

Clear structure gives dancers more options. Once a principle is understood and embodied, it becomes easier to adapt it to new partners, timing, music and situations.

Four-part learning cycle

1. Understand

Identify the movement principle, constraint or decision that actually matters. Build a simple mental model instead of memorizing an isolated answer.

2. Feel

Translate the idea into sensation: timing, weight, direction, tone, connection and body organization. Knowledge has to become physical.

3. Practice

Use focused repetition and feedback to stabilize the skill. Practice is designed around the exact problem rather than repeating an entire pattern indiscriminately.

4. Adapt

Return the skill to real dancing. Change partners, timing, entries, exits, musical context and constraints until the principle remains usable under variation.

Core principles

Movement architecture

Patterns are treated as structures that can be understood, transformed and recombined rather than fixed sequences.

Diagnosis before correction

Training starts by locating the real bottleneck. A visible mistake may come from timing, perception, organization, connection or decision-making.

Deliberate practice

Exercises are narrowed to the skill that needs improvement, with enough repetition and feedback for learning to stabilize.

Kinesthetic learning

Some skills improve faster through guided movement than through explanation. Kinesthetic Practice Sessions emphasize movement, repetition and physical feedback.

Transfer

A skill is not considered learned only because it works in a drill. It must survive different partners, contexts and spontaneous social dancing.

Autonomy

The long-term goal is a dancer who can perceive problems, understand movement and make informed choices without constant external correction.

The idea in one sentence

Teaching should not make the student more dependent on the teacher. It should progressively make the student more capable of learning, adapting and choosing.
